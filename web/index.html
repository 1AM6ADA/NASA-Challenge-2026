A fire record that no longer cares which satellite saw it

Two satellite families have counted burning land since 2000, and their numbers cannot be compared. We rescaled them into one monthly record, so a fire manager can ask whether this month is unusual for this place.

MODIS gives the long view

The MODIS sensors on Terra and Aqua have counted fires at 1 km since late 2000. Both satellites are near the end of their lives.

VIIRS replaces it, and counts more

VIIRS sees fires at 375 m and passes at different times. Over the same ground since 2012 it reports about 2.5× as much burning.

Joining them raw invents a surge

Switch from MODIS to VIIRS in 2012 and the level of the record more than doubles for no reason except the sensor change. A calendar built this way would call every year since then unusual.

First, count the same thing

The unit is one 1 km cell with fire on one day. Several VIIRS pixels inside a cell on the same day count once. Only vegetation fires above a confidence floor are kept.

Learn how one maps to the other

From 2012 both sensors watch the same ground. Each dot is a month. We fit the relationship on 2012 to 2018 and keep 2019 onward as a test the method never saw.

Carry it back to 2000

MODIS history is rescaled into VIIRS terms. Before Aqua launched in mid-2002 only Terra flew, so those months are first lifted to a Terra plus Aqua equivalent. From 2012 the record is VIIRS itself.

The sensor stops mattering

One continuous series. The artificial jump is gone, and what remains is what a manager needs: seasons, bad years and anomalies.

What this is, and what it is not

Estimates before 2012

Early years are MODIS rescaled to VIIRS terms. That assumes the relationship measured since 2012 also held earlier.

Terra-only months

November 2000 to June 2002 rest on one more scaling step and are the least certain part of the record.

Detections, not burned area

Counts depend on cloud, overpass time and fire size, and include agricultural burning.

The newest months

Near-real-time files carry no fire-type flag. We remove cells that the standard archive marks as flares or industry, which catches about 96% of them and also drops about 8% of true vegetation fires.

Coverage

The map and the pipeline cover the whole world; other regions are added as their data arrives.
